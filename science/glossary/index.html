    Glossary¶

    Parameters are explained in Parameter effects and in the API reference. Approach names use kebab-case; the Approach catalog lists them all.

    Approaches¶

    Budget approach
    Allocates one cumulative budget: equal-per-capita-budget, per-capita-adjusted-budget (responsibility and capability) and per-capita-adjusted-gini-budget (plus inequality within countries). The name ends in -budget.
    Pathway approach
    Allocates a value for every year: equal-per-capita, per-capita-adjusted, per-capita-adjusted-gini, and the convergence approaches.
    Convergence approach
    Moves each country from its current emission share to a target share. cumulative-per-capita-convergence and its -adjusted and -gini-adjusted forms preserve the cumulative budget. per-capita-convergence does not preserve it and also includes grandfathering. It is therefore for comparison only.
    See: Convergence
    ECPC
    Equal cumulative per capita: cumulative population from the allocation year sets each country's share of a budget.
    Allocation year
    allocation_year (budget) or first_allocation_year (pathway). Cumulative population from this year sets the shares. It decides whether past emissions create present obligations, so it has no neutral default.
    See: Allocation year

    Concepts¶

    Carbon debt
    The emissions by which a country has exceeded its fair share of atmospheric space, read as an obligation. Can be quantified in tonnes CO2 or monetary terms. Matthews 2016 calculates debts against an equal per capita benchmark; Pelz 2025a introduces a net-zero framing that makes post-peak obligations explicit. Moral grounding: Pickering 2012.
    Cascading biases
    Systematic methodological choices in effort-sharing frameworks that compound to favor wealthy nations. Kartha 2018 identifies three types: scope bias (including cost-effectiveness alongside equity approaches), framing bias (late base years that embed grandfathering), and aggregation bias (equal weighting of ethically unequal approaches).
    See: Allocation Approaches
    CBDR-RC
    Common But Differentiated Responsibilities and Respective Capabilities. UNFCCC foundational principle: all countries share responsibility, but obligations differ based on historical emissions and economic capacity. For the legal interpretation in its normative environment, see Rajamani 2024; for operational interpretations under the Paris Agreement, see Rajamani 2021.
    See: Allocation Approaches
    Egalitarianism
    Ethical tradition grounding equal per capita entitlement to atmospheric space. Agarwal 1991 makes the foundational per-capita argument from an anti-colonial standpoint; Caney 2009 develops the philosophical case for egalitarian allocation of greenhouse gas emission rights.
    See: Allocation Approaches
    Equal per capita
    Each person has an equal entitlement to atmospheric space. Historical accountability enters through an early allocation_year, not through weights.
    See: Allocation approaches
    Grandfathering
    Allocating future entitlements based on current emission shares. Critiqued as lacking an ethical basis: Caney 2009 calls it "morally perverse" and Dooley 2021 finds "virtually no support" for it among moral and political philosophers. per-capita-convergence includes grandfathering elements.
    See: Allocation Approaches
    Historical responsibility
    Past emissions reduce a country's remaining fair share. Two mechanisms put it into practice: an early allocation_year (cumulative accounting, which can give negative allocations) and pre_allocation_responsibility_weight (multiplicative rescaling by emissions in a past window).
    See: The two mechanisms
    Negative allocation
    A remaining allocation below zero: a country's past emissions exceed its allocation. It is an expected result for high historical emitters under an early allocation year. Pelz 2025b report a negative remaining allocation for the EU27 as of 2023 under 1.5 °C budgets. Pelz 2025a and Pelz 2025b read it as calling for the highest possible domestic ambition, net-negative targets through carbon dioxide removal (CDR) and international support for others, and stress minimising the duration and magnitude of overshoot. It does not prescribe timing or mechanism, and reporting it rather than clipping it to zero keeps that information.
    See: Allocation Approaches · From Principle to Code
    Relative and absolute results
    The allocation functions return relative shares that sum to 1. Absolute results multiply them by a global budget or pathway.
    See: Outputs
    Remaining budget
    A country's allocation minus its observed emissions from the allocation year to the last observed year.
    See: Remaining budgets and annual pathways
    Subsistence protection
    Basic needs emissions protected from mitigation burdens. Grounded in Shue 2014's distinction between subsistence and luxury emissions; operationalised in the Greenhouse Development Rights framework (Baer 2009) via a development threshold that excludes both income and emissions of low-income individuals from a country's responsibility and capacity calculations. In fair-shares, implemented via income_floor and Gini adjustments. Shue 2014 also notes that people in poverty need energy rather than emissions rights, which makes the development threshold of Baer 2013 the closer operational concept.
    See: Allocation Approaches

    Data and budget terms¶

    Analysis countries and rest of world (ROW)
    Countries with complete emissions, GDP and population data are allocated individually. The ROW row is the world total minus them.
    See: Analysis countries
    Budget label (TpL)
    The key of a budget in rcbs.yaml, for example 1.7p67. \(T\) is the warming when CO₂ reaches net zero; \(L\) is the likelihood of staying below it. Each source records what \(L\) covers (likelihood_scope) and what \(T\) measures (budget_metric). See: What a budget label means
    Bookkeeping (BM)
    Land-use accounting that estimates only direct human-caused fluxes (deforestation, afforestation, land management). IPCC budgets use it. National inventories also count indirect effects.
    See: Convention gap
    Bunkers
    CO₂ from international aviation and shipping. Global totals include it and national inventories do not, so it leaves the allocatable budget Weber 2026.
    See: Bunker deduction
    Composite category
    all-ghg or all-ghg-ex-co2-lulucf. A remaining carbon budget limits CO₂ only, so the run allocates a CO₂ budget and a non-CO₂ pathway with the same equity approach.
    See: Greenhouse gases
    GCB 2025
    Global Carbon Budget 2025 fossil CO₂ dataset, version 2025v15 (Friedlingstein 2026). Opt-in emissions source gcb-2025, co2-ffi only, 1850-2024. Its world row excludes international bunkers and the 1991 Kuwaiti oil fires. The same file supplies the observed bunker series of every run (bunkers_source: gcb-2025, the default).
    GCB 2025 global workbook
    The Global Carbon Budget 2025 global workbook (gcb-2025-global, manual download). It supplies observed global net CO₂, which expresses each budget from the start of 2020 for its volume-matched scenario set. See: RCB sources and scenario sets
    IGCC 2025 (forster_2026)
    Indicators of Global Climate Change 2025 (Forster 2026). RCB source forster_2026 in rcbs.yaml takes its Table 8 budgets from the start of 2026, for eight temperature and likelihood labels in the defaults. Its scenario-derived terms use the role-matched scenario sets.
    Melo et al. (2026)
    Country-reported NGHGI LULUCF CO₂ timeseries from the LULUCF Data Hub. Two versions are configured. melo-2026-v4 (v4.0.0, 2026 NGHGI release, default) covers 187 countries, 2000-2024. melo-2026 (v3.1.1, opt-in) covers 185 countries, 2000-2023. See: NGHGI-Consistent RCB Corrections
    NGHGI
    National greenhouse gas inventory, reported under the UNFCCC. Its land-use estimates include indirect fluxes such as CO₂ fertilisation, unlike bookkeeping models.
    See: From published budget to allocatable budget
    NGHGI-BM convention gap
    The systematic difference between NGHGI and bookkeeping (BM) LULUCF CO₂ estimates. NGHGI includes indirect effects (CO₂ fertilization of managed forests) that BM excludes, making NGHGI a larger net sink. This package computes a cumulative gap of -91.4 GtCO₂ from 2020 to net-zero CO₂ and -65.6 GtCO₂ from 2024 (median of the AR6 C1 scenarios, melo-2026-v4; the gap does not depend on the emissions source). Weber 2026 reports that in 2024 the adjustment "reduces the global RCB by 63 (41–121) GtCO2 for 1.5 °C (50%)" (Results, "The global NGHGI-consistent RCB"). See: National-inventory convention gap
    Non-CO₂ debt mode
    How past non-CO₂ over-use is settled in a composite category: report-only (reported, no penalty) or co2-debit (subtracted from the remaining CO₂ budget and retired).
    See: Greenhouse gases
    Presets
    Named parameter grids that reproduce a published study (fair_shares.library.presets). esabcc_2023_allocations() returns the ESABCC 2023 / PRR2023 grid that notebook 601 runs. See: Python API
    PRIMAP-hist
    Historical emissions dataset from PIK (Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research). v2.8 (primap-202609, data to 2025, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) is the default emissions source. v2.6.1 (primap-202503, data to 2023, CC BY 4.0) is opt-in. See: Data Sources & Licensing
    RCB
    Remaining carbon budget: the net CO₂ the world can still emit from a start year until CO₂ reaches net zero, for a warming level and a likelihood. Published budgets use bookkeeping land use and include bunkers, so they need corrections before allocation Weber 2026.
    See: Remaining carbon budgets
    Scenario Compass (SCI)
    The Scenario Compass Initiative pathways ensemble (sci-2026 v2.0, or sci-2025 v1.0; manual download). Research use under the embargo terms of its workbook until Riahi et al. is published. fair-shares reads it for scenarios that report bunkers and, opt-in, for the non-CO₂ set. See: Scenario Compass
    Scenario selection rule
    The rule in rcbs.yaml (scenario_selection) that picks the scenarios behind the scenario-derived terms of a budget. The default, role-matched, takes the LULUCF, convention-gap and bunker-window terms from a volume-matched set (cumulative net CO₂ from 2020 to net zero within 50 Gt of the budget from 2020) and the non-CO₂ pathway from a temperature-matched set (median warming at net zero within ±0.05 °C of the budget temperature). ar6-category pairs 1.5p50 with AR6 C1, 2p67 with C3 and 2p50 with C4. See: RCB sources and scenario sets
    Scenario sets (volume-matched, temperature-matched)
    The volume-matched set of a budget holds the scenarios whose cumulative net CO₂ from 2020 to net zero lies within 50 Gt of the budget from 2020. It supplies the land-use, convention-gap and bunker terms. The temperature-matched set holds the scenarios whose median warming at net zero lies within ±0.05 °C of the budget temperature. It supplies the non-CO₂ pathway. See: Role-matched scenario sets
    TimeseriesDataFrame
    A pandas DataFrame indexed by iso3c and unit (plus emission-category for emissions), with one string column per year ("2020").
    WDI Gini
    World Bank Gini index (SI.POV.GINI, from the Poverty and Inequality Platform), source wdi-2025. It is the default Gini source. Each country takes its latest value within 2015-2023. UNU-WIDER WIID (unu-wider-2025) is opt-in.

    Abbreviations¶

    • AR6: IPCC Sixth Assessment Report (2021-2023).
    • BAU: business as usual. Treating deviation from BAU as a cost has been critiqued as inconsistent with CBDR-RC, because fair shares are assessed relative to other parties (Winkler 2018; Rajamani 2021; Pelz 2025b).
    • GDP: gross domestic product.
    • GHG: greenhouse gas. "Kyoto GHG" is the basket of the Kyoto Protocol.
    • IAMC: Integrated Assessment Modeling Consortium, and its data format.
    • IPCC: Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change.
    • ISO 3166-1 alpha-3: three-letter country codes (USA, IND, DEU), the iso3c index level.
    • Mt CO2/yr: megatonnes of CO₂ per year.
    • SSP: Shared Socioeconomic Pathway.
    • TCRE: transient climate response to cumulative emissions.
    • UNFCCC: United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change.
