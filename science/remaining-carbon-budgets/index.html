    Remaining carbon budgets¶

    A remaining carbon budget is the net CO₂ the world can still emit from a start year until CO₂ reaches net zero. Each budget holds warming to a stated level with a stated likelihood. fair-shares takes a published budget and adjusts it to match what countries report in their national inventories. The adjustments follow Weber 2026.

    In short:

    • The library ships budgets from IPCC AR6 WGI, Lamboll et al. (2023) and the Indicators of Global Climate Change updates (Forster et al. 2024 and 2026).
    • Every budget is rebased to start in 2020 with observed emissions.
    • International aviation and shipping (bunkers) are deducted because national inventories report them only as memo items outside their national totals.
    • For fossil CO₂ (co2-ffi), the land-use share of the budget is removed. A projected land sink never adds to the fossil budget.
    • For total CO₂ (co2), land-use CO₂ switches to the national-inventory convention. This lowers the budget.

    Published budgets¶

    data/rcbs/rcbs.yaml holds five sources. All values are in Gt CO₂ of total anthropogenic CO₂: fossil, bunkers and land use. A budget from the start of year \(X\) has baseline_year: X.

    From published budget to allocatable budget¶

    A published budget counts total CO₂ from its own baseline year. Its land-use CO₂ follows the bookkeeping-model (BM) convention. National inventories count land use differently and leave out bunkers. Three adjustments close these gaps:

    1. Rebase to 2020. Observed emissions fill the years from 2020 to the baseline year.
    2. Bunker deduction. Bunker CO₂ from 2020 to net zero leaves the budget.
    3. Land-use term. For co2-ffi, the land-use share of the budget leaves the budget. For co2, land-use CO₂ switches from the BM convention to the national-inventory (NGHGI) convention.
    Equations
    Symbol Definition
    \(\text{RCB}_{\text{BM}}(\text{base})\) Published budget from the baseline year, in BM convention
    \(F_{\text{actual}}(a, b)\) Cumulative observed fossil CO₂ without international bunkers, years \(a\) to \(b\) (e.g. PRIMAP)
    \(L_{\text{BM}}(a, b)\) Cumulative BM land-use CO₂ from the scenario median (AFOLU|Direct), years \(a\) to \(b\)
    \(L_{\text{NGHGI}}(a, b)\) Cumulative observed NGHGI land-use CO₂ (world row of the LULUCF source, Melo et al.), years \(a\) to \(b\)
    \(B(a, b)\) Cumulative international bunker CO₂, years \(a\) to \(b\)
    \(\text{gap}(a, b)\) Cumulative NGHGI minus BM convention gap for CO₂, years \(a\) to \(b\)
    \(\text{NZ}\) Net-zero year (from scenario data)
    \(\text{base}\) Baseline year of the budget (e.g. 2023 for Lamboll, 2020 for AR6 WGI)

    Fossil-only budget (co2-ffi):

    \[ \mathrm{fossil{\_}budget}(2020) = \text{RCB}_{\text{BM}}(\text{base}) + F_{\text{actual}}(2020,\, \text{base}{-}1) + B(2020,\, \text{base}{-}1) - L_{\text{BM}}(\text{base},\, \text{NZ}) - B(2020,\, \text{NZ}) \]

    Total CO₂ budget (co2):

    \[ \mathrm{nghgi{\_}budget}(2020) = \text{RCB}_{\text{BM}}(\text{base}) + F_{\text{actual}}(2020,\, \text{base}{-}1) + B(2020,\, \text{base}{-}1) + L_{\text{NGHGI}}(2020,\, \text{base}{-}1) + \text{gap}(\text{base},\, \text{NZ}) - B(2020,\, \text{NZ}) \]

    with \(\text{gap}(a, b) = \int_{a}^{b} \left[L_{\text{NGHGI}}(t) - L_{\text{BM}}(t)\right] dt\).

    The co2 case has no land-use decomposition. Its land use stays in the budget and changes convention.

    Which data feed each term

    The past uses observed data. Scenario data enter only for future quantities.

    Term Data Coverage

    Bunker rebase and observed bunkers (\(B\)) GCB 2025 (gcb-2025) to 2024
    NGHGI land-use rebase (\(L_{\text{NGHGI}}\), co2 only) Melo et al. (2026) world row: v4.0.0 (melo-2026-v4, default) or v3.1.1 (melo-2026) 2000 to 2024, 187 countries (v4.0.0); 2000 to 2023, 185 countries (v3.1.1)
    BM land-use term (\(L_{\text{BM}}\), co2-ffi only) Gidden et al. AR6 reanalysis, scenario median of AFOLU|Direct 2015 to 2100

    Rebase to 2020¶

    The rebase moves the start of every budget to 2020. A budget published from a later year gets back the observed emissions of 2020 to the year before its baseline:

    • Fossil CO₂. The world fossil series leaves out international bunkers. The PRIMAP world total equals the sum of its countries. The gcb-2025 world row is the GCB global total minus bunkers.
    • Bunkers. The published budget holds bunkers, and the fossil series does not. The output column is rebase_bunkers_mt.
    • Land-use CO₂ (co2 only). Observed land-use CO₂ in the NGHGI convention, from the world row of the active LULUCF source (Melo et al.), which notebook 107 writes. The output column is rebase_lulucf_mt.

    For a budget from 2020 every rebase term is zero.

    Why the co2-ffi rebase adds no land-use CO₂

    The published budget is in the BM convention, so a full rebase would add BM land-use CO₂ for 2020 to \(\text{base}{-}1\). The decomposition would then remove BM land-use CO₂ for 2020 to NZ:

    \[ L_{\text{BM}}(2020,\, \text{base}{-}1) - L_{\text{BM}}(2020,\, \text{NZ}) = -L_{\text{BM}}(\text{base},\, \text{NZ}) \]

    The years 2020 to \(\text{base}{-}1\) cancel. The formula therefore omits land use from the rebase and starts the decomposition at \(\text{base}\). This form suits the pipeline because it needs no observed land-use series in the BM convention.

    Why the co2 rebase adds NGHGI land-use CO₂

    The target quantity is cumulative fossil CO₂ plus NGHGI land-use CO₂ from 2020 to NZ. The published budget holds fossil CO₂, bunkers and BM land-use CO₂ from \(\text{base}\) to NZ.

    For 2020 to \(\text{base}{-}1\) the rebase adds the observed quantities in the target convention: fossil CO₂, bunkers and NGHGI land-use CO₂. For \(\text{base}\) to NZ the convention gap converts the land-use share of the published budget from BM to NGHGI. Each year from 2020 to NZ is converted once.

    Years without observed data

    The rebase needs a value for every year from 2020 to \(\text{base}{-}1\) in each series it adds. When a series ends earlier, each later year takes its last observed value, with a warning that names the source, series, years and value. This fill is a placeholder until observed data are published. rebase_fill_max_years in rcbs.yaml caps the filled years per series (default 1; 0 turns the fill off). A source that needs more is left out of the processed budgets, with one warning listing it.

    Budget Emissions or land-use source Fill
    forster_2026 (baseline 2026) primap-202609 (to 2025) none
    forster_2026 gcb-2025 emissions and bunkers (to 2024) 2025 takes the 2024 value
    forster_2026 primap-202503 (to 2023) two years missing: left out
    forster_2026, co2 melo-2026-v4 (to 2024) 2025 takes the 2024 value
    forster_2026, co2 melo-2026 (to 2023) two years missing: left out
    lamboll_2023, forster_2024 any none

    After the last observed year the bunker deduction uses the bunker share, while a filled rebase year holds the last observed value. The two can count that year differently: 2025 for forster_2026 with gcb-2025 bunkers.

    Bunker deduction¶

    International aviation and shipping count towards global emission totals but national inventories report them only as memo items outside their national totals. The deduction removes them from 2020 to net zero, for every baseline year.

    Observed bunkers count from 2020 to the last observed year \(L\) (gcb-2025 by default, \(L = 2024\)). After \(L\), bunkers are a share \(s\) of the median net CO₂ of the budget's scenario set. The share comes from every scenario that reports bunkers, balanced across model families (Bunker share). With the default sources \(s = 0.072\). The 1.5 °C (50%) budgets thus deduct 4.96 Gt of observed bunkers (2020 to 2024) plus 0.072 times 225 to 293 Gt of set net CO₂. A set with more net CO₂ after \(L\) takes more bunkers.

    Equation and why the rebase adds bunkers
    \[ B(2020,\, \text{NZ}) = \sum_{t=2020}^{L} B_{\text{obs}}(t) + s \cdot \underset{i \in \text{set}}{\text{median}}\; C_i(L{+}1,\, t_{\text{nz},i}) \]

    \(C_i\) is the cumulative net CO₂ of scenario \(i\) of the budget's volume-matched set (or AR6 category) from the start of \(L{+}1\) to its net-zero crossing.

    The two bunker terms count each bunker year once. The bunker rebase restores the published budget to a total-CO₂ budget from 2020. The deduction then removes every bunker year from 2020 to NZ:

    \[ B(2020,\, \text{base}{-}1) - B(2020,\, \text{NZ}) = -B(\text{base},\, \text{NZ}) \]

    The years 2020 to \(\text{base}{-}1\) cancel, and the net deduction covers the years that the published budget holds. The result is the budget from 2020 without any bunker emissions, for every baseline year.

    The function is bunker_deduction_from_share() in src/fair_shares/library/utils/data/nghgi.py.

    Land-use term for fossil CO₂¶

    A published budget includes land-use CO₂. A fossil-only budget removes the land-use share from the baseline year to net zero. No observation covers these future years, so the term comes from scenarios. Each scenario of the budget's volume-matched set (Role-matched scenario sets) gives its cumulative AFOLU|Direct flux from the baseline year to its own net-zero crossing. The term is the median across these scenarios. Notebook 104 stores one median per baseline year as bm_lulucf_median_from in rcb_scenario_adjustments.yaml.

    A precautionary cap (default on) keeps a projected land sink from adding to the fossil budget. A net land source still reduces it. The sink relies on uncertain future reforestation, so the cap sets a net sink to zero. precautionary_lulucf: false in the adjustments config turns the cap off for sensitivity analysis.

    Equation
    \[ L_{\text{BM}}^{\text{capped}} = \max\!\left(0,\; \underset{i}{\text{median}}\int_{\text{base}}^{t_{\text{nz},i}} L_{\text{BM},i}(t)\,dt\right) \]

    The structure follows Weber 2026 (Methods, Eqs. 2 and 3). Differences from Weber et al. (2026) lists where the package departs from it.

    rcb_scenario_adjustments.yaml also holds convention_gap_median_from for every budget. Only co2 allocations read it.

    National-inventory convention gap for total CO₂¶

    Bookkeeping models (e.g. BLUE, OSCAR) estimate only direct human-caused land-use fluxes: deforestation, afforestation and land management. National inventories (NGHGIs) also count indirect effects, such as CO₂ fertilisation of managed forests and climate-driven changes in soil carbon. NGHGI land-use CO₂ is therefore a larger net sink than the BM estimate. The global difference is 5 to 7 Gt CO₂ per year, mainly from CO₂ fertilisation on managed land (Weber 2026).

    A total CO₂ budget keeps land use and switches it to the NGHGI convention. The convention gap covers the years of the published budget, from the baseline year to net zero. Weber 2026 (Methods, Eqs. 2 and 3) uses the same split: the correction starts at the reference year of the budget, and earlier years take observed NGHGI land-use CO₂. The gap is negative, so it reduces the allocatable budget. Notebook 104 stores one median per baseline year in convention_gap_median_from. The output column is correction_lulucf_convention_mt.

    How the gap is computed per scenario

    The per-scenario gap \(\text{Gap}_i\) runs from the baseline year \(\text{base}\) to the net-zero crossing of the scenario. It has two segments.

    Observed (\(\text{base} \leq t \leq \mathrm{splice{\_}year}\)): observed NGHGI land-use CO₂ (the same for all scenarios) minus the BM flux of scenario \(i\). The splice year is the last year of the NGHGI data (2023 for melo-2026, 2024 for melo-2026-v4). NGHGI data come from Melo et al. and the BM flux from the Gidden et al. AR6 reanalysis (AFOLU|Direct). Other sources of the same quantities can take these roles.

    \[ \text{Gap}_{i,\text{obs}} = \int_{\text{base}}^{\min(\text{splice},\, t_{\text{nz},i})} \left[\text{NGHGI}_{\text{actual}}(t) - \text{BM}_{\text{Direct},i}(t)\right] dt \]

    Future (\(t > \mathrm{splice{\_}year}\)): only the indirect component of scenario \(i\) (CO₂ fertilisation and other passive fluxes), because the direct components cancel. It comes from the Gidden et al. AR6 reanalysis (AFOLU|Indirect).

    \[ \text{Gap}_{i,\text{future}} = \int_{\max(\text{base},\, \text{splice})}^{t_{\text{nz},i}} \text{Indirect}_{i}(t)\, dt \]

    The total is \(\text{Gap}_i = \text{Gap}_{i,\text{obs}} + \text{Gap}_{i,\text{future}}\). The median runs over the volume-matched set or the AR6 category of the budget, and a budget takes the median of its own baseline year. Each year counts in full, and the net-zero year adds its fraction (Full-year accounting).

    Edge cases:

    • A baseline year after the splice year leaves the observed segment empty. The gap is then the indirect component from \(\text{base}\).
    • A scenario that reaches net zero before \(\text{base}\) has a gap of zero.
    • The years 2020 to \(\text{base}{-}1\) sit outside the gap. The rebase adds observed NGHGI land-use CO₂ for them.

    The per-scenario function is convention_gap_from_baseline() in src/fair_shares/library/utils/data/rcb.py.

    Differences from Weber et al. (2026)¶

    The package follows the structure of Weber 2026 (Methods, Eqs. 2 and 3), with five differences.

    The five differences
    1. Scenario sets. Weber et al. use AR6 C1 scenarios for 1.5 °C (50%) and C3 for 2 °C (66%). The package uses these categories for the AR6 sources and role-matched sets for the other sources.
    2. Statistic. Weber et al. take the mean across scenarios for the land-use and bunker terms. The package takes the median.
    3. BM side. Weber et al. adjust the Gidden et al. values with the difference between the Global Carbon Budget mean of bookkeeping models and NGHGI-reported values for 2020 to 2023 (Methods, "Calculating a NGHGI-consistent global RCB"). The package uses the scenario AFOLU|Direct flux, also for the observed years from the baseline year to the splice year, and holds no observed BM series. The gap is a per-scenario quantity: each scenario has its own AFOLU|Direct pathway, net-zero year and indirect flux. One multi-model average would collapse this variation and break the integrate-then-median method.
    4. Bunker term. Weber et al. take the bunker pathways of the C1 or C3 scenarios that report aviation and maritime (8 and 9 scenarios). The package applies a family-balanced bunker share of net CO₂ to the scenario set of every budget, AR6 categories included.
    5. Accounting. The package counts full years to an interpolated net-zero crossing for every term.

    Allocation years other than 2020¶

    The allocatable budget runs from 2020. The allocation_year parameter moves the start. An earlier year adds the world's historical emissions before 2020. A later year subtracts the emissions already used.

    The world series matches the adjusted budget. It holds fossil CO₂ without bunkers and, for total CO₂, NGHGI land-use CO₂:

    \[ E_{\text{world}}(t) = E_{\text{fossil}}(t) + \text{LULUCF}(t) \]

    NGHGI land-use data start in 2000: LULUCF Data Hub v4.0.0 (melo-2026-v4, default, 2000 to 2024) or v3.1.1 (melo-2026, opt-in, 2000 to 2023). Categories with land-use CO₂ therefore start in 2000 or later (Start-year limits). An allocation year before the first year of the world series raises an error that names the first available year. calculate_budget_from_rcb then works the same way for co2-ffi and co2. build_world_co2_timeseries() and the preprocessing notebooks build the same series.

    Why land-use CO₂ starts in 2000

    The official NGHGI land-use data begin in 2000. Earlier NGHGI estimates exist, such as historical extensions of Melo et al. Extending to 1990 would splice heterogeneous datasets, and the BM-to-NGHGI transition around 1990 shows a large jump. The package performs no NGHGI/BM splicing. We plan to extend coverage when validated pre-2000 NGHGI land-use data become available.

    Budgets outside the defaults¶

    Some published labels stay out of the default rcbs.yaml, because their scenario sets are too thin:

    • 1.5 °C budgets with high likelihoods (forster_2024, forster_2026, ar6_wg1_2021). Fewer than 10 volume-matched scenarios.

    To run one, add its label and published value to data/rcbs/rcbs.yaml. Each of its scenario sets must hold at least min_scenarios scenarios.

    Labels left out, with published values and counts
    Source Label Published (Gt) Reason
    forster_2024 1.5p67, 1.5p83 150, 100 6 and 3 volume-matched scenarios
    forster_2026 1.5p67, 1.5p83, 1.5p90 80, 30, 10 8, 5 and 3 volume-matched scenarios
    ar6_wg1_2021 1.5p83 300 6 volume-matched scenarios

    Worked example¶

    The two boxes trace one budget from 2020 and one from 2023 through every adjustment, with values from pipeline runs on the default sources.

    A budget from 2020: IPCC AR6 WGI, 1.5 °C at 50%

    IPCC AR6 WGI, 1.5 °C at 50% (ar6_2020, 1.5p50): 500 Gt CO₂ from 2020. It takes AR6 category C1: 70 scenarios, all reaching net zero. Values come from pipeline runs on the default sources: PRIMAP-hist v2.8 (primap-202609), LULUCF Data Hub v4.0.0 (melo-2026-v4) and gcb-2025 bunkers. Each row is rounded, and the totals use unrounded values.

    Published budget (total CO₂) 500 Gt 500 Gt
    Rebase (fossil, bunkers, land use) 0 (base = 2020) 0 (base = 2020)
    Land-use term / convention gap 0 (BM sink capped) -91.4 Gt (gap, 2020 to NZ)
