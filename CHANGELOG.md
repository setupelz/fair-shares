## [Unreleased]

### Added

- Column dictionary `conf/output_columns.yaml`: the meaning and unit of every output column. Allocation runs and notebook 405 write `columns.csv` beside their outputs, and the 405 IAMC files carry a `columns` sheet; a column without an entry stops the write. The docs page `Output columns` is generated from the same file (`python -m fair_shares.library.utils.output_columns`), and a test keeps the two equal.

### Changed

- **Breaking.** `pre_allocation_responsibility_per_capita` defaults to `True` in every adjusted budget and pathway approach: responsibility uses cumulative emissions per person. With total emissions (the old default) the factor scaled with country size, so small countries gained for being small and a region's total depended on how it split into countries. Runs that set the parameter, including the ESABCC 2023 preset, are unchanged; set `False` for the old behaviour.
- Notebook 405 IAMC meta: the text column `co2-budget` is `co2-budget-definition`, so it no longer shares a name with the numeric `co2-budget` of the CSV.

## [0.6.0] — 2026-10-20

### Added

- Notebook 405: `EMISSION_CATEGORY = "co2-ffi"`, a fossil and industry CO2 budget for a model without land use, with its land-use condition: per area, budget and approach, the bookkeeping land-use CO2 allowance (the area's share of the global allocation times the land-use term the budget removed; zero when the precautionary cap counts a projected sink as zero) and observed land-use CO2 for 2020 to 2024 on the bookkeeping and inventory bases, with the indirect gap. Written to `<REGION>_land_use_condition.csv`; a markdown cell sets out when a net sink is needed.
- Notebook 405 reports each member country as well as the region, in the printed tables, the tidy CSV and the IAMC files (members under their ISO3 code); the run stops if the members do not sum to the region.
- `member_targets()` and `land_use_condition()` in `python_api`.

### Changed

- Notebook 405 defaults: responsibility is per capita (`pre_allocation_responsibility_per_capita=True`), emissions per person since 2015. Absolute responsibility, the library default, divides by a country's total emissions, which favoured small countries for their size (Malta 656 t CO2 per person against Germany's 15 under 1.7°C 50%) and made a region's total depend on how it splits into countries. EU27 budget from 2020, total CO2, 1.7°C 50% and 2°C 90%: responsibility 3.7 and 4.1 Gt (was 10.9 and 12.3 Gt with absolute responsibility). Approach labels end in `rpc`.
- Notebook 405 defaults add responsibility (0.4, per capita since 2015) and capability (0.6, development threshold 7,500 dollars per person) combined, from 2020: 2.5 and 2.8 Gt for the EU27 (was 4.1 and 4.6 Gt with absolute responsibility).
- Notebook 405 defaults: the responsibility and capability approaches allocate from 2020 and count responsibility for emissions from 2015 (was: allocated from 2015, responsibility from 1990), so they hold a region accountable from the same year as equal cumulative per capita from 2015.

## [0.5.0] — 2026-10-19

### Added

- LULUCF source `gcb-2025-luc`: Global Carbon Budget 2025 national land-use change emissions v1.0 (ICOS, CC BY 4.0, doi:10.18160/GCP-2025), per country 1850-2024, the mean of the bookkeeping models BLUE, OSCAR and LUCE (Tg C converted to Mt CO2). Manual tier: the file sits behind the ICOS licence page; `fair-shares fetch-data` prints the steps.
- A LULUCF source now declares its accounting `basis` (`nghgi` or `bookkeeping`) in `data_parameters`, and the run follows it. With `gcb-2025-luc` (bookkeeping), `co2` and `all-ghg` are on the model basis: country history uses bookkeeping land use, the global budget takes no NGHGI convention-gap correction and is rebased with the workbook's global bookkeeping land use, and the AR6 scenario CO2 and Kyoto gases are used as reported rather than NGHGI-adjusted. Budget rows record `lulucf_basis`. Loaders in `utils/data/lulucf.py`.
- Notebook `405_one_region_remaining_budget`: fair-share targets for one region of countries (EU27 preset), for a model that covers that region only. It allocates the global budget between countries for one RCB source and a list of (warming level, likelihood) pairs and sums the member countries. Settings in three cells (your model; budget and region; advanced). The user chooses `co2` (default) or `all-ghg`, the land-use basis (`model`, default, or `inventory`), their model's variable names, and their first model time step and period length; the CO2 budget is given from the first year of that period. Output: a cumulative CO2 budget and, for `all-ghg`, an annual non-CO2 pathway (AR6 GWP100) per non-CO2 debt mode, also as model-period averages, as a tidy CSV and IAMC files.
- `filter_to_anchors()`, `regional_remaining_budgets()`, `regional_targets()`, `regional_observed_emissions()`, `shift_to_period_start()` and `period_means()` in `python_api`. Region sums stop when a member has no row, was moved into rest of world, or when two configurations share every column that `compute_remaining_budgets()` keeps (a grid varying `cumulative_end_year`, a functional form or `capability_reference_year`), which would otherwise be summed together. Under `co2-debit`, a region's non-CO2 over-use is summed over the members that over-used. `shift_to_period_start()` adds back observed emissions inside the model's first period, or subtracts the years before it, holding unobserved years at the last observed value and recording them.

### Changed

- Names that said NGHGI but hold the active LULUCF source's data are basis-neutral: `emiss_<category>_nghgi_timeseries.csv` is `emiss_<category>_lu_timeseries.csv`; the adjustments key `lulucf_nghgi` is `lulucf_world`; RCB columns `rcb_2020_nghgi_mt` and `correction_lulucf_nghgi_mt` are `rcb_2020_mt` and `correction_lulucf_convention_mt`; `build_nghgi_world_co2_timeseries` is `build_world_co2_timeseries`; `NGHGI_CORRECTED_CATEGORIES` is `LULUCF_CATEGORIES`; notebook 107 is `107_derive_lulucf_categories`; `lulucf_metadata.yaml` keys `nghgi_*` are `lulucf_*` and it records `basis`. Output folders rebuild once.

## [0.4.0] — 2026-10-16

### Added

- Provenance stamp per output folder, `output/<source-id>/.provenance_stamp.json`. It hashes the package version, the composed run config, the preprocessing notebooks, the `Snakefile`, the preprocessing library modules, the packaged config files, `data/rcbs/rcbs.yaml` and the registry checksums of the active input files. `setup_data()` used to reuse any folder whose files existed, so a code or config change left stale outputs in place. It now reruns all preprocessing for the folder with `snakemake --forceall` when the stamp is missing or differs, and logs the changed components. `FAIR_SHARES_TRUST_CACHE=1` or `setup_data(trust_cache=True)` reuses a folder without the check. Existing folders carry no stamp, so each rebuilds once. Rule `compose_config` also declares the packaged config files and a YAML target file as inputs.
- Scenario source registry `src/fair_shares/conf/scenario_sources.yaml`. An entry declares the world variables (CO2, AFOLU Direct and Indirect, Kyoto gases), the CO2 series of the volume match, the bunker variables, the metadata columns (net-zero year, median and 67th-percentile warming at net zero, category), the model families and the budget terms it supplies. Entries: `ar6` (Gidden et al. 2023, every term), `sci` and `scenariomip` (bunker share and non-CO2 set).
- Data source `ipcc_ar6_bunkers` (manual tier): world aviation and maritime CO2 and `Emissions|CO2` of every AR6 scenario that reports them, read anonymously from the public AR6 Scenarios Database with `fetch_ar6_bunkers()`. The database licence restricts redistribution of substantial parts, so the file is not shipped.
- Data source `ipcc_ar6_climate` (manual tier): world harmonised-infilled CO2, reported CO2 and the MAGICCv7.5.3 GSAT median and 67th percentile of every AR6 scenario, read with `fetch_ar6_climate_series()`. Notebook 104 derives the net-zero year and the warming at net zero of every AR6 scenario from it.
- PRIMAP-hist v2.8 (Gütschow and Pflüger 2026, doi:10.5281/zenodo.22876287, data 1750-2025, latest years partly extrapolated) as the emissions source `primap-202609`. Its licence is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. The Zenodo record states it, while the metadata file inside the record says CC BY-NC 4.0, and the registry records the stricter terms. The registry marks it not redistributable, and `fair-shares cite` and each run's `CITATIONS.md` list its non-commercial, share-alike terms. One parameterised notebook, `101_data_preprocess_emiss_primap`, serves both PRIMAP releases through a `notebook` key in `data_sources_unified.yaml`.
- LULUCF Data Hub v4.0.0 (gap-filled NGHGI, 187 countries, 2000-2024) as the LULUCF source `melo-2026-v4`.
- Global Carbon Budget 2025 national fossil CO2 (1850-2024) as the opt-in emissions source `gcb-2025`, `co2-ffi` only. Its world row excludes international bunkers and the 1991 Kuwaiti oil fires, and its runs take bunkers from the same file. The pipeline asks an emissions source only for the categories it declares.
- RCB sources `forster_2026` (IGCC 2025, Table 8, from 2026) and `ar6_wg1_2021` (AR6 WGI Table SPM.2, from 2020), with 1.5, 1.7 and 2°C budgets. A source whose rebase needs more filled years than `rebase_fill_max_years` is skipped with a warning.
- Opt-in coverage rule for analysis countries: a `coverage` block on an emissions source (`emissions_recorded_before`, `population_from`). `gcb-2025` sets it to 1990 and 1850, so a country needs an emissions record before 1990 and population from 1850. Ten countries with otherwise complete data join rest-of-world. Eight have their first GCB record in 1990 or later (AND, FSM, LSO, MHL, NAM, PLW, TLS, TUV), and the population data start Ireland in 1950 and Macao in 1860. A `co2-ffi` run therefore holds 167 analysis countries. `country_data_coverage_summary.csv` names the failed test in `coverage_rule_failed`, and notebook 101 saves `emiss_co2-ffi_first_recorded_year.csv`. Sources without the block, including both PRIMAP releases, keep their country set.
- `rebase_fill_max_years` in `rcbs.yaml` (default 1). When a world series of the rebase (fossil CO2, bunkers, LULUCF) ends before the year before the budget baseline, each later year takes the last observed value, up to this number of years, with a warning that names the source, the series, the years and the value. The fill is a placeholder until observed data are published. With `gcb-2025` (observed to 2024) `forster_2026` rebases with 2025 filled. With PRIMAP-hist v2.6.1 (observed to 2023) it needs two years and stays skipped. PRIMAP-hist v2.8 is observed to 2025 and needs no fill. 0 turns the fill off.
- Emission category `co2` with a `co2-ffi`-only emissions source and an active LULUCF source, for example `gcb-2025` with `melo-2026-v4` and target `rcbs`. Notebook 107 derives `co2` as `co2-ffi` plus national-inventory LULUCF and skips the non-CO2 categories that such a source cannot supply. Without a LULUCF source the configuration still raises an error.
- `capability_window=(start, end)` on the four adjusted per-capita approaches (budget and pathway). Capability is one value per country, cumulative GDP over cumulative population for the inclusive window, and it reproduces Pelz et al. (2025, PNAS, SI Eq. 2) and Weber et al. (2026, Eq. 18) with the power form and exponent 0.5. The Gini adjustment applies to each window year before the sum. Setting it together with `capability_reference_year` raises a `ConfigurationError`. Results record it in the `capability-window` column as `start-end`.
- Role-matched scenario sets per budget (`src/fair_shares/library/utils/data/scenario_sets.py`). The BM LULUCF term (`co2-ffi`), the convention gap (`co2`) and the net CO2 behind the bunker term use a volume-matched set: scenarios that reach net-zero CO2 by 2100 and whose cumulative net CO2 from 2020 to net zero lies within `volume_tolerance_gt` (50 Gt) of the budget from 2020. The non-CO2 pathway uses a temperature-matched set: scenarios that reach net zero by 2100 and whose median warming at net zero lies within `temperature_half_width` (0.05 °C) of the budget temperature, for every likelihood. A set below `min_scenarios` (10) raises a `ConfigurationError` naming the budget. `rcb_scenario_adjustments.yaml` is keyed by `source:label` and records per budget the label as published, `likelihood_scope`, `budget_metric`, the budget from 2020, the rule, size, median cumulative CO2 and median and 67th-percentile warming at net zero of both sets, and their overlap and volume gap.
- Data source `gcb-2025-global` (manual tier, CC BY 4.0, doi:10.18160/GCP-2025): the Global Carbon Budget 2025 global workbook. Observed global net CO2 (fossil minus cement carbonation plus bookkeeping land use) of 2020 to the year before the baseline expresses each budget from 2020.
- Scenario source `sci`: the Scenario Compass Initiative pathways ensemble. The configured release is v2.0 (data registry `sci-2026`, doi:10.5281/zenodo.23232097, `data/scenarios/sci-2026-v2.0/`), and the `version` key selects v1.0 (`sci-2025`, doi:10.5281/zenodo.18598251) instead. Both are manual downloads, gitignored and not redistributable. Their terms allow research use under the Nature editorial and embargo rules until Riahi et al. is published, and `fair-shares cite` shows them. v2.0 holds 1,977 scenarios, and 92 of its scenarios that report bunkers and reach net zero enter the bunker share. Its non-CO2 term is the infilled Kyoto gases (AR6-GWP100) minus CO2.
- Scenario source `scenariomip` (data registry `scenariomip-cmip7`, manual tier, not redistributable): the ScenarioMIP-CMIP7 global workbook v0.1 (doi:10.5281/zenodo.22296051), read from `data/scenarios/scenariomip-v0.1/` (gitignored). The licence permits reuse for scientific analysis and prohibits redistribution of substantial parts. It is in the default bunker share, where its 42 scenarios that reach net zero count, and off by default for the non-CO2 set. `model_families` adds its models (GCAM and COFFEE as new families).
- Net zero in the AR6 WGIII convention (`net_zero_years()` and `warming_at_net_zero()` in `scenario_sources.py`). A scenario's net-zero year is the first full year from 2020 at which its harmonised CO2, linear between reported years, is at or below zero. A scenario without one counts as reaching net zero when its harmonised or reported CO2 falls below 100 Mt CO2 per year by 2100, in the first such year (AR6 WGIII Table SPM.2, note j). The warming at net zero is the median and 67th-percentile GSAT of that year, and a scenario without net zero by 2100 takes the 2100 warming. Notebook 104 derives these columns for every AR6 scenario from `ipcc_ar6_climate`, because the AR6 metadata hold the 2100 warming for 72 scenarios that reach net zero only under the 100 Mt count. On the other 519 AR6 scenarios with a net-zero year, the derived warming matches the AR6 metadata within 0.013 °C. Scenario Compass and ScenarioMIP take the same columns from their own CO2 and GSAT series.
- `non_co2.scenario_sources` in `rcbs.yaml` (default `[ar6]`): the scenario sources pooled into the temperature-matched non-CO2 set. A source must list `non-co2` in its `terms`. Notebook 104 reads it, and a listed SCI or ScenarioMIP source adds its vetted scenarios that are not AR6 carry-overs (84 for Scenario Compass v2.0, 33 of which reach net zero). Land-use and convention-gap terms stay AR6 only, because SCI and ScenarioMIP carry no NGHGI land-use split.
- RCB labels in the defaults: `lamboll_2023` `1.5p50`, `2p50`, `2p66`, `2p90`; `forster_2024` `1.5p50`, `1.7p50`, `1.7p67`, `1.7p83`, `2p50`, `2p67`, `2p83`; `ar6_2020` `1.5p50`, `2p50`, `2p67`, `2p83`; `forster_2026` `1.5p50`, `1.7p50`, `1.7p67`, `1.7p83`, `1.7p90`, `2p50`, `2p67`, `2p83`, `2p90`; `ar6_wg1_2021` `1.5p50`, `1.5p67`, `1.7p50`, `1.7p67`, `1.7p83`, `2p50`, `2p67`, `2p83`. Each source records `likelihood_scope` (`tcre-only` for AR6 WGI and IGCC, `tcre-zec-esf` for Lamboll et al.) and `budget_metric` (warming at net zero, and peak warming for Lamboll et al.). Six labels stay out because their volume-matched sets hold fewer than `min_scenarios` scenarios (3 to 8): `forster_2024` `1.5p67`, `1.5p83`, `forster_2026` `1.5p67`, `1.5p83`, `1.5p90` and `ar6_wg1_2021` `1.5p83`. The `rcbs.yaml` header lists them with their published values and counts.

### Changed

- An analysis country without a value in a year that one allocation reads now joins rest of world for that allocation only. `run_allocation()` works out the years each input is read for the configuration (cumulative population from the allocation year, responsibility windows, capability snapshots and windows, current emission shares for convergence). It moves the countries with gaps into ROW and records them in the new `moved-to-row` result column and `moved_to_row` attribute. ROW stays the world total minus the remaining analysis countries. The remaining-budget and pathway steps of the Python API add the moved countries' observed emissions, base-year emissions and population to ROW. A CO2 budget part and its non-CO2 part that moved different countries raise an `AllocationError`. With the default OWID population, allocations that read population before 1950 place Ireland in ROW, and allocations that read population before 1860 also place Macao there.
- The non-CO2 debt mode `free-rider` is renamed `report-only` in the Python API, notebook 601, outputs (`nonco2-debt-mode`) and docs. The CO2 budget stays as it is and past non-CO2 over-use is reported. `free-rider` still works as a deprecated alias. It maps to `report-only` and emits a `DeprecationWarning`. An unknown mode raises a `ValueError`.
- The Gini approaches normalise capability by the median of the unadjusted indicator (GDP per capita or GDP before the income floor). The median of the floor-adjusted capability falls as the floor rises. A country held at the `max_gini_adjustment` cap then saw its relative capability rise and its factor fall, a reversal instead of a plateau. Now each country's capability factor is non-decreasing in the floor and constant once the cap binds. Results change only for `per-capita-adjusted-gini-budget`, `per-capita-adjusted-gini` and `cumulative-per-capita-convergence-gini-adjusted` with `income_floor > 0`. Approaches without the Gini adjustment, and Gini runs at floor 0, are unchanged. On the default data (budget from 2020, capability weight 1.0) India's share over floors 0, 2,500, 5,000, 7,500, 10,000, 15,000 and 20,000 is 12.6, 6.1, 7.0, 10.3, 14.0, 13.4 and 13.1%. The shares are not monotonic because of the cap: the poorest countries reach it first and their factors jump, and India's own capability then falls until it reaches the cap.
- **Breaking:** `calculate_relative_adjustment` raises an `AllocationError` that names the countries with a missing capability or responsibility value. It filled them with 0 after normalising, which gives `asinh(0) ** -exponent`, an infinite factor. New arguments `reference` (fixed normalisation reference) and `indicator` (name in the message).
- `create_gini_lookup_dict` keys on the allocation's `group_level`, so the Gini approaches run on region-level inputs. Gini data must carry that level as an index level or column, and otherwise an `AllocationError` names the missing level.
- Docs: `max_gini_adjustment` binds for low-income countries and keeps the inverse capability factor finite. The 0.8 default binds for 8, 43, 54, 69 and 86 of 178 rows (177 countries and rest of world) at floors 2,500, 7,500, 10,000, 15,000 and 20,000. At a floor of 10,000 India's share is 14.0% with the 0.8 cap and 0.5% without a cap.
- **Breaking:** every budget takes its scenario-derived terms from role-matched sets (see Added). `ar6_2020` and `ar6_wg1_2021` set `scenario_selection: ar6-category` and pair `1.5p50` with C1, `2p67` with C3 and `2p50` with C4 for every term (AR6 WGIII Annex III). Their other labels use the role-matched sets. The map `2p83 -> C2` is removed. C2 returns warming to 1.5°C after a high overshoot, so its warming peaks above 1.5°C and C2 stays outside every budget label. The rules `warming-at-net-zero`, `peak-warming`, `peak-warming-band` and `end-of-century` and `bunkers.fallback` raise a `ConfigurationError`. With the default sources, the adjusted `ar6_2020` `1.5p50` budget is 474.0 Gt for `co2-ffi` and 382.6 Gt for `co2`, after a bunker deduction of 26.0 Gt.
- **Breaking:** `forster_2024` and `ar6_2020` label their 67% budgets `2p67` (was `2p66`, quantile 0.67). `2p66` still reads for these sources with a `DeprecationWarning`. Lamboll et al. publish 66%, so `lamboll_2023` keeps `2p66`. AR6 category medians in the scenario outputs carry C1 `1.5C`/0.5, C3 `2C`/0.67 and C4 `2C`/0.5 (was C1, C2 `2C`/0.83, C3 `2C`/0.66).
- **Breaking:** the bunker deduction counts observed bunkers from 2020 to the last observed year L, then a share s of the median net CO2 of the budget's volume-matched set (or AR6 category) from L+1 to net zero. Every scenario of `bunkers.scenario_sources` (default `[ar6, sci, scenariomip]`) that reports international aviation and shipping and reaches net zero gives a ratio: its cumulative bunkers over its own net CO2 (`Emissions|CO2`) from L+1 to its own net zero. Net zero follows the AR6 convention on the scenario's own net CO2, so the climate emulator behind a source does not enter. A scenario carried over from another listed source counts once. No historical vetting applies to these scenarios. s is family-balanced: the median of the per-model-family medians, with the families listed in `model_families` of `scenario_sources.yaml` (an unlisted model is a `ConfigurationError`). A family counts only with at least `bunkers.min_runs_per_family` (3) scenarios, and the excluded families are recorded per budget. AR6 bunkers are 0.6 x Aviation plus Maritime (Weber et al. 2026), and Scenario Compass and ScenarioMIP report international bunkers directly. With `gcb-2025` bunkers (L = 2024), 156 scenarios that report bunkers reach net zero: 22 from AR6, 92 from Scenario Compass and 42 from ScenarioMIP. Eight families count: IMAGE 0.072 (57 scenarios), REMIND 0.071 (46), MESSAGE 0.074 (13), WITCH 0.051 (12), GCAM 0.078 (10), AIM 0.060 (7), COFFEE 0.033 (6) and IMACLIM 0.106 (5). GEM-E3, McKinsey, POLES (2 each) and LUT-ESTM (1) are excluded. s = 0.072 (0.0718), the median over all 156 scenarios is 0.072 and the interquartile range is 0.060 to 0.081. AR6 category budgets apply the same s to the net CO2 of their category set. Fewer than `bunkers.min_reporters` (10) scenarios or `bunkers.min_families` (3) families after the per-family filter raise a `ConfigurationError`. Before, the median bunker pathway of the set's scenarios that report bunkers was integrated to each scenario's net zero, with a `nearest` fallback.

For `co2` and `all-ghg`, the parameter grid checks `pre_allocation_responsibility_year` against the first year of the responsibility emissions frame it receives.

Before, every budget took the C1, C2 or C3 median by climate assessment and quantile. Notebook 104 writes per-budget medians to `scenarios_{category}_by_budget_timeseries.csv`, the non-CO2 rows carry the RCB source key as `source`, and the pairing matches on source, climate assessment and quantile. A budget without a non-CO2 partner raises an error. Cumulative world non-CO2 from 2020 to 2050, harmonised to PRIMAP-hist v2.8, is 308.7 Gt CO2e at 1.5°C, 331.3 at 1.7°C and 367.7 at 2°C for every likelihood, and 300.2 for C1, 334.9 for C3 and 362.6 for C4.

The series is linear between its reported years, the net-zero year is the interpolated zero crossing, and a cumulative sums the full years from its start to the year before the crossing plus the fraction of the crossing year (`net_zero_crossing()`, `integrate()` in `rcb.py`). The BM LULUCF term now integrates each scenario from the baseline year (`bm_lulucf_median_from`), and `lulucf_shift_median_*.csv` is no longer written.
- **Breaking:** the default observed bunker source is `gcb-2025` (Global Carbon Budget 2025, to 2024) for every emissions source, and it moves to the default fetch tier. `gcb-2024` stays available as `bunkers_source: gcb-2024`. With `primap-202609` the rebase covers `forster_2026` (bunkers observed to 2024, 2025 filled).
- `fair-shares cite` lists the sources behind the budget terms for budget runs: `ipcc_ar6_gidden`, `ipcc_ar6_bunkers`, `ipcc_ar6_climate` and `gcb-2025-global`. It adds each other scenario source of `bunkers.scenario_sources` whose configured release is present, under that release's registry name (`sci-2026` and `scenariomip-cmip7` with the defaults), and shows the special terms of both.

### Fixed

- `load_iamc_data` checks that every required variable has a value for every required region at both the allocation start year and the budget end year. A region whose series started later was back-filled from its first year by the annual expansion. The function now raises an `IAMCDataError` that lists the missing variable, region and year combinations and points to notebook 400 for regional history.
- Per-capita responsibility summed population with missing years skipped, so a country whose population series starts inside the window got a per-capita value from partial population. Ireland, with population from 1950, was overstated about 3.3 times for 1850-1989. The responsibility, capability-window and budget population sums now raise an `AllocationError` that names the countries and missing years when called directly, and the orchestrated route moves such countries into rest of world first.
- `compose_config` did not declare the packaged `data_sources_unified.yaml` as an input, so an existing output folder reused its stale `config.yaml` after a source changed (for example a new `coverage` block) until the run used `--forcerun compose_config`. The rule now re-runs when that file changes.
- Adjusted budgets of RCB sources with a baseline after 2020 were too low. The rebase to 2020 added world fossil emissions, which exclude international bunkers, and the bunker deduction covered 2020 to net zero, so the bunkers of 2020 to the year before the baseline were deducted and never added. The rebase now adds them (new output column `rebase_bunkers_mt`). With the default sources it adds 2.7 Gt to every budget of `lamboll_2023` (baseline 2023), 3.8 Gt to `forster_2024` (baseline 2024) and 6.2 Gt to `forster_2026` (baseline 2026). `ar6_2020` and `ar6_wg1_2021` (baseline 2020) are unchanged. `process_rcb_to_2020_baseline` takes the bunker series as `world_bunker_emissions`.
- Adjusted `co2` budgets of RCB sources with a baseline after 2020 were too low. The rebase to 2020 adds observed LULUCF in the national-inventory (NGHGI) convention for 2020 to the year before the baseline, and the BM-to-NGHGI convention gap also started in 2020, so the conversion for those years was applied twice. The convention gap now runs from the baseline year of the source to net zero (Weber et al. 2026, Eqs. 2-3). Notebook 104 writes one median gap per baseline year (`convention_gap_median_from` in `rcb_scenario_adjustments.yaml`, which replaces `convention_gap_median`), so existing outputs need a re-run of notebook 104. Budgets with a 2020 baseline and every `co2-ffi` budget are unaffected. The argument `actual_bm_lulucf_emissions` of `load_and_process_rcbs` and `process_rcb_to_2020_baseline` is now `world_nghgi_lulucf_emissions`, because the series is the NGHGI world row and the pipeline holds no observed bookkeeping-model LULUCF series.
- `build_nghgi_world_co2_timeseries` subtracted bunkers from a world fossil series that already excludes them. World `co2` is now fossil plus LULUCF, as in notebook `100_data_preprocess_rcbs`, and the function no longer takes `bunker_ts`. The error reached `run_rcb_preprocessing` and notebook `106_generate_pathways_from_rcbs`. Budget files written by notebook 100 did not contain it.
- `compute_bunker_deduction` treated 2023 as the last observed bunker year for every source. It now reads the last year from the bunker data.
- `calculate_budget_from_rcb` summed only the years present when the allocation year preceded the first year of the world emissions series (for example `co2`, which starts in 2000, with allocation year 1990). It now raises an error that names the missing years and the first available year.
- `calculate_budget_from_rcb` raised no error when the allocation year was after 2020 and the world emissions ended earlier, and summed only the years present. It now raises an error that names the missing years and the last available year. A year with a missing value counts as missing in this check, in the pre-2020 check and in the rebase to 2020, which earlier summed NaN-padded years as zero.
- For `co2`, the pipeline could run the scenario notebook (104) before the LULUCF notebook (107) and then write a zero convention gap. The scenario rule now depends on notebook 107. `process_rcb_to_2020_baseline` raises an error for a `co2` budget with a baseline after 2020 when it receives no inventory LULUCF series, where it used a zero LULUCF rebase term before. A budget with baseline 2020 needs no series.
- `import fair_shares.library.config` and `import fair_shares.library.config.models` failed with a circular import when they were the first import in a process.
- A budget label without a numeric temperature raised a bare `ValueError`, and it now raises a `ConfigurationError` that names the label. `rebase_fill_max_years` must be a non-negative integer.
- The OWID population loader tolerates the column renames of the unversioned OWID export (#21).
- Input validation of the allocation functions required the index levels `iso3c` and `unit`, so an allocation with another `group_level` (for example `r11eu27`) raised an index error. `AllocationInputs` and the dataset validators now take `group_level` and `unit_level` from the function, and result objects accept the allocation's group level. Shares match those of the same data indexed by `iso3c`.
- Input validation required country emissions in the allocation year, although the pre-allocation responsibility window `[pre_allocation_responsibility_year, allocation_year)` excludes it. Budget and per-capita pathway approaches now require one emissions year inside the window, and the error names the window. The convergence approaches still require emissions at the first allocation year, which they read.
- `convert_budget_config_to_pathway` and `derive_pathway_allocations` compared keys in snake_case only, so a kebab-case budget config kept `cumulative-end-year` and `allocation-year` in the derived pathway config. Keys are now normalised to snake_case first.

## [0.3.0] — 2026-08-03

### Added

- `fair-shares fetch-data`: downloads every input dataset from source, verifies pinned checksums, records provenance in `data/PROVENANCE.md`. Missing files also fetch automatically on first use.
- Python API reference (`docs/api/python-api.md`) for pip-only users.
- Root `CONTRIBUTING.md`. This changelog.
- World Bank WDI Gini index (`SI.POV.GINI`) as a Gini source, with `analysis/gini_source_comparison.py` reporting the coverage and value differences against WIID.

### Changed

- **Breaking:** `pip install fair-shares` installs only the allocation library. Notebook/pipeline tools moved to the `pipeline` extra, documentation tools to `docs`.
- The package works when installed outside a clone. Data/output locations resolve: explicit argument → `FAIR_SHARES_DATA_DIR`/`FAIR_SHARES_OUTPUT_DIR` → existing per-user directory → repo root.
- Config files moved into the package (`src/fair_shares/conf/`); repo-root `conf/` removed.
- **Breaking:** the default Gini source is now World Bank WDI (`wdi-2025`), which is CC-BY-4.0. WIID stays available as `active_gini_source=unu-wider-2025` but is opt-in, and outputs built on it cannot be redistributed under CC BY 4.0. Output directory names change, so existing WIID runs are not overwritten. The two sources give materially different capability-based allocations — WDI/PIP is consumption-based for most low- and middle-income countries.
- **Breaking:** analysis-country membership no longer depends on Gini availability. A country with complete emissions, GDP and population is now in the analysis even without a Gini value, and receives the analysis-country mean (`general.gini_missing_policy: fallback-mean`, or `strict` to refuse). The country set grows by 9 on the standard sources; `country_data_coverage_summary.csv` gains a `gini_imputed` column.
- Gini source config replaces the unused `world_key` and `gini_year` keys with `selection` and `year_window`, both of which the notebooks read.

### Deprecated

- `project_root=` in `calculate_allocation_timeseries` — use `data_dir=`/`output_dir=`. Still works, warns.

### Removed

- Four unused data sources (IMF WEO, WID.world, Taiwan GDP override, Grassi 2023 LULUCF) with their notebooks and config entries.

### Fixed

- Global Carbon Budget citation pointed at a DOI that does not resolve; now the correct paper DOI (10.5194/essd-17-965-2025) plus data-product DOI (10.18160/GCP-2024).
- Licence statements corrected: WIID is CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO, UN/OWID population is mixed-terms, CMIP7 is CC-BY-SA-4.0 (author-confirmed).
- The Python API's preprocessing path wrote `country_gini_stationary.csv` without a Rest-of-World row, unlike the notebook path. Both now use the same code.
- Notebook `100_data_preprocess_rcbs` passed the removed `project_root=` argument to `load_and_process_rcbs`, so the RCB pipeline could not run.
- The Gini-adjusted approaches ignored Gini entirely when `capability_reference_year` named a year before `allocation_year`: the capability snapshot was read from the unfiltered inputs without the adjustment, on both the budget and pathway side. Results on that parameterisation change — on the standard sources, China's share of a `per-capita-adjusted-gini-budget` allocation moves from 10.8% to 5.3% and India's from 23.2% to 28.4%.
