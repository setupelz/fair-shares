## [Unreleased]

### Added

- Scenario source registry `src/fair_shares/conf/scenario_sources.yaml`. An entry declares the world variables (CO2, AFOLU Direct and Indirect, Kyoto gases), the CO2 series of the volume match, the bunker variables, the metadata columns (net-zero year, median and 67th-percentile warming at net zero, category) and the budget terms it supplies. Entries: `ar6` (Gidden et al. 2023, every term) and `sci` (bunker reporters only).
- Data source `ipcc_ar6_bunkers` (manual tier): world aviation and maritime CO2 of every AR6 scenario, read anonymously from the public AR6 Scenarios Database with `fetch_ar6_bunkers()`. The database licence restricts redistribution of substantial parts, so the file is not shipped.
- PRIMAP-hist v2.8 (Gütschow and Pflüger 2026, doi:10.5281/zenodo.22876287, data 1750-2025, latest years partly extrapolated) as the emissions source `primap-202609`. Its licence is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (the Zenodo record; the metadata file inside the record says CC BY-NC 4.0, and the registry records the stricter terms). The registry marks it not redistributable, and `fair-shares cite` and each run's `CITATIONS.md` list its non-commercial, share-alike terms. One parameterised notebook, `101_data_preprocess_emiss_primap`, serves both PRIMAP releases through a `notebook` key in `data_sources_unified.yaml`. It reproduces the `primap-202503` emissions files byte for byte.
- LULUCF Data Hub v4.0.0 (gap-filled NGHGI, 187 countries, 2000-2024) as the LULUCF source `melo-2026-v4`.
- Global Carbon Budget 2025 national fossil CO2 (1850-2024) as the opt-in emissions source `gcb-2025`, `co2-ffi` only. Its world row excludes international bunkers and the 1991 Kuwaiti oil fires, and its runs take bunkers from the same file. The pipeline asks an emissions source only for the categories it declares.
- RCB sources `forster_2026` (IGCC 2025, Table 8, from 2026) and `ar6_wg1_2021` (AR6 WGI Table SPM.2, from 2020), with 1.5, 1.7 and 2°C budgets. A rebase with missing emission years raises an error, and the pipeline skips that source with a warning.
- Opt-in coverage rule for analysis countries: a `coverage` block on an emissions source (`emissions_recorded_before`, `population_from`). `gcb-2025` sets it to 1990 and 1850, so a country needs an emissions record before 1990 and population from 1850. Eight countries with their first GCB record in 1990 or later (AND, FSM, LSO, MHL, NAM, PLW, TLS, TUV) and Macao (population from 1950) join rest-of-world, which leaves 168 analysis countries in a `co2-ffi` run. `country_data_coverage_summary.csv` names the failed test in `coverage_rule_failed`, and notebook 101 saves `emiss_co2-ffi_first_recorded_year.csv`. Sources without the block, including `primap-202503`, keep their country set and outputs.
- `rebase_fill_max_years` in `rcbs.yaml` (default 1). When a world series of the rebase (fossil CO2, bunkers, LULUCF) ends before the year before the budget baseline, each later year takes the last observed value, up to this number of years, with a warning that names the source, the series, the years and the value. The fill is a placeholder until observed data are published. With `gcb-2025` (observed to 2024) `forster_2026` rebases with 2025 filled. With PRIMAP-hist v2.6.1 (observed to 2023) it needs two years and stays skipped. PRIMAP-hist v2.8 is observed to 2025 and needs no fill. 0 turns the fill off.
- Emission category `co2` with a `co2-ffi`-only emissions source and an active LULUCF source, for example `gcb-2025` with `melo-2026-v4` and target `rcbs`. Notebook 107 derives `co2` as `co2-ffi` plus national-inventory LULUCF and skips the non-CO2 categories that such a source cannot supply. Without a LULUCF source the configuration still raises an error.
- `capability_window=(start, end)` on the four adjusted per-capita approaches (budget and pathway). Capability is one value per country, cumulative GDP over cumulative population for the inclusive window, and it reproduces Pelz et al. (2025, PNAS, SI Eq. 2) and Weber et al. (2026, Eq. 18) with the power form and exponent 0.5. The Gini adjustment applies to each window year before the sum. Setting it together with `capability_reference_year` raises a `ConfigurationError`. Results record it in the `capability-window` column as `start-end`.
- Role-matched scenario sets per budget (`src/fair_shares/library/utils/data/scenario_sets.py`). The BM LULUCF term (`co2-ffi`), the convention gap (`co2`) and the net CO2 behind the bunker term use a volume-matched set: scenarios that reach net-zero CO2 by 2100 and whose cumulative net CO2 from 2020 to net zero lies within `volume_tolerance_gt` (50 Gt) of the budget from 2020. The non-CO2 pathway uses a temperature-matched set: a harmonised-infilled net-zero year and median warming at net zero within `temperature_half_width` (0.05 °C) of the budget temperature, for every likelihood. A set below `min_scenarios` (10) raises a `ConfigurationError` naming the budget. `rcb_scenario_adjustments.yaml` is keyed by `source:label` and records per budget the label as published, `likelihood_scope`, `budget_metric`, the budget from 2020, the rule, size, median cumulative CO2 and median and 67th-percentile warming at net zero of both sets, and their overlap and volume gap.
- Data source `gcb-2025-global` (manual tier, CC BY 4.0, doi:10.18160/GCP-2025): the Global Carbon Budget 2025 global workbook. Observed global net CO2 (fossil minus cement carbonation plus bookkeeping land use) of 2020 to the year before the baseline expresses each budget from 2020.
- Scenario source `sci` (data registry `sci-2025`, manual tier, not redistributable): the Scenario Compass Initiative pathways ensemble v1.0 (doi:10.5281/zenodo.18598251), read from `data/scenarios/sci-2025-v1.0/` (gitignored). Its terms allow research use under the Nature embargo rules until Riahi et al. is published; `fair-shares cite` shows them. Its metadata carry no warming at net zero, so it supplies bunker reporters only: 17 vetted scenarios report international bunkers, 3 reach net zero.
- RCB labels: `lamboll_2023` `2p90` (500 Gt), `forster_2024` `1.7p50`, `1.7p67`, `1.7p83`, `2p83`, `ar6_2020` `2p83`, `forster_2026` `1.7p83`, `1.7p90`, `2p83`, `2p90`, `ar6_wg1_2021` `1.5p67`, `1.7p83`, `2p83`. Each source records `likelihood_scope` (`tcre-only` for AR6 WGI and IGCC, `tcre-zec-esf` for Lamboll et al.) and `budget_metric` (warming at net zero; peak warming for Lamboll et al.). Left out with their counts in the `rcbs.yaml` header: every `2p50` (no scenario that reaches net zero at that volume reports bunkers; C4 holds none), `forster_2024` `1.5p67`, `1.5p83`, `forster_2026` `1.5p67`, `1.5p83`, `1.5p90` and `ar6_wg1_2021` `1.5p83` (3 to 8 volume-matched scenarios).

### Changed

- **Breaking:** every budget takes its scenario-derived terms from role-matched sets (see Added). `ar6_2020` and `ar6_wg1_2021` set `scenario_selection: ar6-category` and pair `1.5p50` with C1, `2p67` with C3 and `2p50` with C4 for every term (AR6 WGIII Annex III); their other labels use the role-matched sets. The map `2p83 -> C2` is removed (C2 returns warming to 1.5°C after high overshoot). The rules `warming-at-net-zero`, `peak-warming`, `peak-warming-band` and `end-of-century` and `bunkers.fallback` raise a `ConfigurationError`. Relative to the warming-at-net-zero sets of the previous run (`primap-202609`, `melo-2026-v4`, 2026-10-16), and together with the accounting and bunker changes below, adjusted `co2-ffi` budgets change by -24.6 to +5.3 Gt (`lamboll_2023` 2p66 988.6 to 964.0, `ar6_2020` 1.5p50 469.9 to 469.3, 2p67 1088.1 to 1087.9) and `co2` budgets by -39.2 to +22.4 Gt (`ar6_2020` 1.5p50 376.5 to 377.9, 2p67 915.4 to 937.8; `lamboll_2023` 1.5p50 243.2 to 264.8; `ar6_wg1_2021` 1.7p67 566.1 to 526.9).
- **Breaking:** `forster_2024` and `ar6_2020` label their 67% budgets `2p67` (was `2p66`, quantile 0.67). `2p66` still reads for these sources with a `DeprecationWarning`. Lamboll et al. publish 66%, so `lamboll_2023` keeps `2p66`. AR6 category medians in the scenario outputs carry C1 `1.5C`/0.5, C3 `2C`/0.67 and C4 `2C`/0.5 (was C1, C2 `2C`/0.83, C3 `2C`/0.66).
- **Breaking:** the bunker deduction counts observed bunkers from 2020 to the last observed year L, then a share s of the median net CO2 of the budget's volume-matched set (or AR6 category) from L+1 to net zero. Every scenario of `bunkers.scenario_sources` (default `[ar6, sci]`) that reports international aviation and shipping and reaches net zero gives a ratio: its cumulative bunkers over its own net CO2 (`Emissions|CO2`) from L+1 to its own net zero. No historical vetting applies to these reporters. s is family-balanced: the median of per-model-family medians, with the families listed in `model_families` of `scenario_sources.yaml` (an unlisted model is a `ConfigurationError`). A family counts only with at least `bunkers.min_runs_per_family` (3) reporters; excluded families are recorded per budget. AR6 bunkers are 0.6 x Aviation plus Maritime (Weber et al. 2026); SCI reports international bunkers directly. `ar6_bunkers_world.csv` now also holds `Emissions|CO2` of the reporting scenarios (re-fetch with `fetch_ar6_bunkers()`). With `gcb-2025` bunkers (L = 2024): 64 reporters (25 AR6, 39 SCI); counted families IMAGE 0.073 (n=37), REMIND 0.088 (17), IMACLIM 0.106 (5); excluded WITCH 0.076 (2), McKinsey 0.097 (2), LUT-ESTM 0.191 (1); s = 0.088 (pooled median over all 64: 0.077, interquartile range 0.072 to 0.095). AR6 category budgets apply the same s to the net CO2 of their category set. Fewer than `bunkers.min_reporters` (10) reporters or `bunkers.min_families` (3) families after the per-family filter raise a `ConfigurationError`. Before, the median bunker pathway of the set's reporters was integrated to each scenario's net zero, with a `nearest` fallback. Against the warming-at-net-zero sets, bunker deductions change by -5.4 to +18.0 Gt (`lamboll_2023` 1.5p50 30.1 to 24.7 Gt, 2p66 61.9 to 79.7 Gt, `ar6_2020` 1.5p50 30.1 to 30.7 Gt).
- The processed RCB files carry the provenance of each budget: `published_label`, `likelihood_scope`, `budget_metric`, `scenario_selection`, `budget_2020_gt`, `terms_set_rule`, `terms_set_n`, `nz_year_median`, `non_co2_set_rule`, `non_co2_set_n`, `bunker_share`, `bunker_share_q25`, `bunker_share_q75`, `bunker_share_from`, `bunker_share_n`, `bunker_share_by_source`, `bunker_share_models`, `bunker_set_co2_after_mt`. Notebook 104 writes `scenario_set_{source}_{label}.csv` per budget (members, net-zero crossings, net CO2 from each possible start year) and `bunker_share_reporters.csv`.
- **Breaking:** the default emissions source is PRIMAP-hist v2.8 (`primap-202609`, data to 2025), and the default LULUCF source is the LULUCF Data Hub v4.0.0 (`melo-2026-v4`, data to 2024). `fair-shares fetch-data` downloads both; `primap-202503` (v2.6.1) and `melo-2026` (v3.1.1) stay available as opt-in sources. Results from a default run include PRIMAP-hist v2.8 data and so carry its CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 terms: non-commercial use and share-alike. For CC BY results, set the emissions source to `primap-202503` or `gcb-2025`. Output directory names change with the sources, so existing runs are not overwritten.
- Notebook 601 runs on the new defaults (`primap-202609`, `melo-2026-v4`). Its remaining budgets are netted through 2025 and reported from 2026. In the ESABCC 2023 preset, the all-GHG responsibility entry is split into an EPC-base and an ECPC-base entry, and the ECPC-base runs set `cumulative_end_year=2050`, as PRR2023 and the `co2-ffi` runs do. These runs cumulated population to the end of the population series before.
- Allocation years start at 1850 (was 1900). With `capability_reference_year` set, the adjusted budget approaches need GDP at the reference year only, so `allocation_year` can precede the GDP series. For `co2` and `all-ghg`, the parameter grid checks `pre_allocation_responsibility_year` against the first year of the responsibility emissions frame it receives.
- Composite RCB targets (`all-ghg`, `all-ghg-ex-co2-lulucf`) take the non-CO2 pathway of each budget from its temperature-matched set, or from its AR6 category. Before, every budget took the C1, C2 or C3 median by climate assessment and quantile. Notebook 104 writes per-budget medians to `scenarios_{category}_by_budget_timeseries.csv`, the non-CO2 rows carry the RCB source key as `source`, and the pairing matches on source, climate assessment and quantile. A budget without a non-CO2 partner raises an error. Cumulative non-CO2 2020-2050 (harmonised to PRIMAP-hist v2.8) is 315.2 Gt CO2e at 1.5°C, 331.8 at 1.7°C and 366.6 at 2°C for every likelihood, 300.2 for C1 and 334.9 for C3 (the warming-at-net-zero sets gave 312.1, 334.8 and 309.6 at 1.7°C p67, and 361.1).
- **Breaking:** every cumulative to net zero uses one accounting. Budgets run from the start of 2020, so every year counts in full: the series is linear between its reported years, the net-zero year is the interpolated zero crossing, and a cumulative sums the full years from its start to the year before the crossing plus the fraction of the crossing year (`net_zero_crossing()`, `integrate()` in `rcb.py`). This covers the volume of the scenario match, the BM LULUCF term, the convention gap and the bunker term; before, they summed whole years to the first year at or below zero. The BM LULUCF term now integrates each scenario from the baseline year (`bm_lulucf_median_from`); `lulucf_shift_median_*.csv` is no longer written. On identical scenario sets the change alone shifts each scenario's cumulative CO2 to net zero by 0.0 Gt (median; the fraction of the net-zero year replaces the whole year), the BM LULUCF term by +1.7 to +6.9 Gt and the convention gap by +1.0 to +2.1 Gt.
- **Breaking:** the default observed bunker source is `gcb-2025` (Global Carbon Budget 2025, to 2024) for every emissions source; it moves to the default fetch tier. `gcb-2024` stays available as `bunkers_source: gcb-2024`. With `primap-202609` the rebase now covers `forster_2026` (bunkers observed to 2024, 2025 filled).
- `fair-shares cite` lists the sources behind the budget terms for budget runs: `ipcc_ar6_gidden`, `ipcc_ar6_bunkers`, `gcb-2025-global`, and `sci-2025` when the bunker share reads it.

### Fixed

- `compose_config` did not declare the packaged `data_sources_unified.yaml` as an input, so an existing output folder reused its stale `config.yaml` after a source changed (for example a new `coverage` block) until the run used `--forcerun compose_config`. The rule now re-runs when that file changes.
- Adjusted budgets of RCB sources with a baseline after 2020 were too low. The rebase to 2020 added world fossil emissions, which exclude international bunkers, and the bunker deduction covered 2020 to net zero, so the bunkers of 2020 to the year before the baseline were deducted and never added. The rebase now adds them (new output column `rebase_bunkers_mt`). Every adjusted budget of `lamboll_2023` (baseline 2023) rises by 2,822 Mt CO2 and every adjusted budget of `forster_2024` (baseline 2024) by 3,959 Mt CO2, for `co2-ffi` and `co2`, with PRIMAP emissions and `gcb-2024` bunkers. `ar6_2020` (baseline 2020) is unchanged. `process_rcb_to_2020_baseline` takes the bunker series as `world_bunker_emissions`.
- Adjusted `co2` budgets of RCB sources with a baseline after 2020 were too low. The rebase to 2020 adds observed LULUCF in the national-inventory (NGHGI) convention for 2020 to the year before the baseline, and the BM-to-NGHGI convention gap also started in 2020, so the conversion for those years was applied twice. The convention gap now runs from the baseline year of the source to net zero (Weber et al. 2026, Eqs. 2-3). Notebook 104 writes one median gap per baseline year (`convention_gap_median_from` in `rcb_scenario_adjustments.yaml`, which replaces `convention_gap_median`), so existing outputs need a re-run of notebook 104. With PRIMAP emissions and `melo-2026` LULUCF, the `1.5p50` budget of `lamboll_2023` rises from 222,434 to 241,477 Mt CO2 and that of `forster_2024` from 206,034 to 230,905 Mt CO2. `ar6_2020` (baseline 2020) and every `co2-ffi` budget are unchanged. The argument `actual_bm_lulucf_emissions` of `load_and_process_rcbs` and `process_rcb_to_2020_baseline` is now `world_nghgi_lulucf_emissions`: the series is the NGHGI world row, and the pipeline holds no observed bookkeeping-model LULUCF series.
- `build_nghgi_world_co2_timeseries` subtracted bunkers from a world fossil series that already excludes them (871 Mt CO2 in 2020; 20,770 Mt over 2000-2019). World `co2` is now fossil plus LULUCF, as in notebook `100_data_preprocess_rcbs`, and the function no longer takes `bunker_ts`. The error reached `run_rcb_preprocessing` and notebook `106_generate_pathways_from_rcbs`; budget files written by notebook 100 did not contain it.
- `compute_bunker_deduction` treated 2023 as the last observed bunker year for every source. It now reads the last year from the bunker data, so `gcb-2025` runs use the observed 2024 value and extrapolate the 2024 rate. Runs with `gcb-2024` bunkers give the same deduction as before.
- `calculate_budget_from_rcb` summed only the years present when the allocation year preceded the first year of the world emissions series (for example `co2`, which starts in 2000, with allocation year 1990). It now raises an error that names the missing years and the first available year.
- `calculate_budget_from_rcb` raised no error when the allocation year was after 2020 and the world emissions ended earlier, and summed only the years present. It now raises an error that names the missing years and the last available year. A year with a missing value counts as missing in this check, in the pre-2020 check and in the rebase to 2020, which earlier summed NaN-padded years as zero.
- For `co2`, the pipeline could run the scenario notebook (104) before the LULUCF notebook (107) and then write a zero convention gap. The scenario rule now depends on notebook 107. `process_rcb_to_2020_baseline` raises an error for a `co2` budget with a baseline after 2020 when it receives no inventory LULUCF series; it used a zero LULUCF rebase term before. A budget with baseline 2020 needs no series.
- `import fair_shares.library.config` and `import fair_shares.library.config.models` failed with a circular import when they were the first import in a process.
- A budget label without a numeric temperature under the peak-warming band rule raised a bare `ValueError`; it now raises a `ConfigurationError` that names the label. `rebase_fill_max_years` must be a non-negative integer.
- The OWID population loader tolerates the column renames of the unversioned OWID export (#21).

## [0.3.0] — 2026-08-03

### Added

- `fair-shares fetch-data`: downloads every input dataset from source, verifies pinned checksums, records provenance in `data/PROVENANCE.md`. Missing files also fetch automatically on first use.
- Python API reference (`docs/api/python-api.md`) for pip-only users.
- Root `CONTRIBUTING.md`. This changelog.
- World Bank WDI Gini index (`SI.POV.GINI`) as a Gini source, with `analysis/gini_source_comparison.py` reporting the coverage and value differences against WIID.

### Changed

- **Breaking:** `pip install fair-shares` installs only the allocation library. Notebook/pipeline tools moved to the `pipeline` extra, documentation tools to `docs`.
- The package works when installed outside a clone. Data/output locations resolve: explicit argument → `FAIR_SHARES_DATA_DIR`/`FAIR_SHARES_OUTPUT_DIR` → existing per-user directory → repo root.
- Config files moved into the package (`src/fair_shares/conf/`); repo-root `conf/` removed.
- **Breaking:** the default Gini source is now World Bank WDI (`wdi-2025`), which is CC-BY-4.0. WIID stays available as `active_gini_source=unu-wider-2025` but is opt-in, and outputs built on it cannot be redistributed under CC BY 4.0. Output directory names change, so existing WIID runs are not overwritten. The two sources give materially different capability-based allocations — WDI/PIP is consumption-based for most low- and middle-income countries.
- **Breaking:** analysis-country membership no longer depends on Gini availability. A country with complete emissions, GDP and population is now in the analysis even without a Gini value, and receives the analysis-country mean (`general.gini_missing_policy: fallback-mean`, or `strict` to refuse). The country set grows by 9 on the standard sources; `country_data_coverage_summary.csv` gains a `gini_imputed` column.
- Gini source config replaces the unused `world_key` and `gini_year` keys with `selection` and `year_window`, both of which the notebooks read.

### Deprecated

- `project_root=` in `calculate_allocation_timeseries` — use `data_dir=`/`output_dir=`. Still works, warns.

### Removed

- Four unused data sources (IMF WEO, WID.world, Taiwan GDP override, Grassi 2023 LULUCF) with their notebooks and config entries.

### Fixed

- Global Carbon Budget citation pointed at a DOI that does not resolve; now the correct paper DOI (10.5194/essd-17-965-2025) plus data-product DOI (10.18160/GCP-2024).
- Licence statements corrected: WIID is CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO, UN/OWID population is mixed-terms, CMIP7 is CC-BY-SA-4.0 (author-confirmed).
- The Python API's preprocessing path wrote `country_gini_stationary.csv` without a Rest-of-World row, unlike the notebook path. Both now use the same code.
- Notebook `100_data_preprocess_rcbs` passed the removed `project_root=` argument to `load_and_process_rcbs`, so the RCB pipeline could not run.
- The Gini-adjusted approaches ignored Gini entirely when `capability_reference_year` named a year before `allocation_year`: the capability snapshot was read from the unfiltered inputs without the adjustment, on both the budget and pathway side. Results on that parameterisation change — on the standard sources, China's share of a `per-capita-adjusted-gini-budget` allocation moves from 10.8% to 5.3% and India's from 23.2% to 28.4%.
